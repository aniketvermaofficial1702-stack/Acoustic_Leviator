· Technical feasibility

How 40 kHz sound becomes a hand

1 Emit

Transducers send 40 kHz ultrasound, far above human hearing.

2 Reflect

The wave returns and forms a standing wave.

3 Trap

Small objects settle at pressure nodes. λ = 343 ÷ 40,000 ≈ 8.6 mm, so nodes sit λ/2 ≈ 4.3 mm apart.

· Technical feasibility

Built from off-the-shelf parts

Controller

Arduino-class board makes precise 40 kHz timing.

→

Driver

Amplifies the signal to power the transducers.

→

Transducers

Array aimed at a reflector forms the standing wave.

→

Object

Foam beads, droplets or parts hover at the nodes.

Limits, and how we handle them

Small, light objects only → scale with array size. Heating → duty-cycle control. Air-based → fine for lab work.

Cost target

≈ ₹5,000

· Scalability & sustainability

Designed to grow, built to last

Scale

Standard parts make it easy to replicate. More transducers mean more traps, and larger arrays handle more samples. Same core design from classroom to lab.

Sustain

No consumables, no contact wear, low power draw. Reusable by design, with few parts to replace.

Revenue paths

Lab modules · education kits · paid pilots with research labs
